Basic Care for All Birds

  • Cage: large enough to spread wings and make short flights; away from drafts, the kitchen and smoke.
  • Hygiene: clean the cage floor daily, wash food and water dishes daily, disinfect the cage weekly.
  • Water: always clean and fresh, with a bath two to three times a week.
  • Light and sleep: 10–12 hours of dark, quiet sleep and indirect daylight.
  • Temperature: avoid extreme heat and sudden cold; keep it stable.
  • Interaction: social birds such as parrots need daily time, toys and enrichment.
  • Safety: avoid burnt non-stick (PTFE) cookware, perfumes and cleaning sprays; their fumes are toxic to birds.
⚠️ Never feed birds: avocado, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, large amounts of onion or garlic, or apple seeds and fruit pits.

Health & Nutrition

🥚 Proteins and Supplements

  • Protein sources: boiled egg (with crushed shell for calcium), mealworms and dried crickets for insect-eaters, cooked legumes and sprouted seed mixes.
  • Calcium: cuttlebone and mineral blocks, especially important for laying females.
  • Vitamins: vitamin A (carrots, sweet potato, leafy greens), vitamin D3 (unfiltered sunlight), and water-soluble vitamins under veterinary guidance.
  • During molting: extra protein and amino acids (such as methionine) support feather regrowth.
  • Grit: small stones help some birds, like pigeons and chickens, grind grain.

💊 Common Illnesses and Treatments

  • Diarrhea and gut infections: treated by cause (bacterial or parasitic), usually with antibiotics or anticoccidials prescribed by a vet.
  • Internal parasites: dewormers (such as ivermectin or fenbendazole, depending on species) at vet-set doses.
  • Mites and lice: bird-safe sprays or drops, plus a deep clean of the cage.
  • Fungal disease (e.g. aspergillosis): antifungals by prescription; prevent with good ventilation and clean food.
  • Vitamin deficiency: corrected with a varied diet and suitable supplements.
🩺 Important: this is general information only. Never give any medicine or antibiotic without advice from an avian vet; a wrong dose can kill a small bird. See a vet at once for fluffed feathers, lethargy, loss of appetite, labored breathing or abnormal droppings.
